# What we can learn from your Upwork channel (lessons transfer to off-Upwork)

> **Scope note:** This analysis is drawn from your **Upwork/GetMany** history — that was the only
> place you had a real body of applications to study. Upwork is your *other* system, NOT this one.
> "Ben," "Kickstarter," etc. are Upwork proposals — ignore them as targets here. What's valuable
> are the **transferable lessons** below, which we apply to your off-Upwork business applications.

Bottom line lesson: **the leak is the follow-up + underselling, not sourcing.**
Warm replies die before they become a call — and the same mistakes would sink off-Upwork apps too.

## The evidence

### 1. You ARE getting replies. They're not converting.
- Weekly (Jun 22–28): 10 proposals sent, **40% reply rate**, 60% view rate. (Jun 15–21 swung to 0% — very volatile.)
- Reply ≠ interview. The replies are coming; the *conversion of replies to calls* is where it breaks.

### 2. The smoking gun — the "Ben / Kickstarter" reply
Ben wrote back **interested** ("I like the work") and asked three dead-simple questions:
1. **Rough ballpark price?**
2. **Where are you based?** (Thailand or California)
3. **Is it you on camera, or do you sub it out?**

These are the *same three questions almost every client asks.* If they aren't answered **fast and up front**, the lead stalls — and GetMany's own note says a reply within 1 hour = **3x more likely to hire.** Warm leads like Ben are the callbacks you're "not getting" — they're just stalling at question #1.

### 3. Your cover letters are templated AND understated
- Every one opens **"Hey [Name], love this brief!"** — clients see this opener constantly; it reads mass-applied.
- Worse, it's **inaccurate/understated**: "on-camera spokesperson for **200+ B2B brands since 2015**." Your real, stronger, accurate credentials are **20+ years, Top Rated Plus (Top 1%), 500+ projects, 250+ brands incl. Microsoft/Facebook/Ahrefs/Sony.** You're selling yourself *short* and inconsistently.
- They **don't pre-empt the obvious questions** (price, is-it-you, location), so clients stall exactly like Ben did.

### 4. Some connects are wasted on poor-fit gigs
e.g. "Egocentric Video Data Collection" — not an on-camera presenter role. Spend connects on real fits.

### 5. Off-Upwork, you're leaving the biggest pile untouched
Your LinkedIn alerts deliver **30+ "on-camera" roles a day** (plus "video spokesperson," "Video lead") — and you're applying to ~none of them because the off-Upwork pipeline wasn't running. That's the volume of quality at-bats you're missing.
> ⚠️ Your "Host" alert is polluted with restaurant-host jobs (Texas Roadhouse, RH, True Food Kitchen). Ignore those — tighten the alert to "on-camera host."

## The fixes (what changes)

1. **Answer the 3 questions before they're asked.** Every cover letter + reply pre-empts: price range, "yes it's me on camera," location/timezone. See `answers-clients-always-ask.md`.
2. **Reply to warm replies within the hour**, using `playbooks/conversation.md` Message 1 → book a call. This alone should lift your callback rate the most.
3. **Use accurate, consistent credentials** (see `resume.md`) — stop underselling ("200+ since 2015" → your real 20 yrs / 500+ / 250+ brands / Top 1%).
4. **Drop the "love this brief!" opener** — lead with the company/role + one specific proof point (per `README.md` cover-letter rules).
5. **Attach/link a video** every time — you're an on-camera pro; a text-only pitch wastes your edge.
6. **Work the LinkedIn feed daily** (the off-Upwork batch surfaces the real fits + scores them).

## Do this first (today)
Open your Upwork messages and **reply to every warm thread** (Ben and the others) with a price range + "yes, it's me on camera" + a booking link + free-sample offer. Those are callbacks waiting to happen.
