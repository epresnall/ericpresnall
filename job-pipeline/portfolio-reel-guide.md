# Portfolio & demo reel guide — what on-camera employers actually want to see

Research-backed (Backstage, SAG-AFTRA, NewsLab, SportsTVJobs, Mort Meisner, ReelArc). This
is the "what videos/portfolio do I need?" answer for landing off-Upwork on-camera roles.

---

## The one rule that matters most
**Lead with your single strongest clip.** Every source agrees: agents and hiring managers
decide in the first 5–10 seconds. Don't build to your best moment — open on it. If the first
clip doesn't grab them, they never reach the rest.

---

## What you actually need (priority order)

### 1. A tight master reel — 60–90 seconds (NOT 5 minutes)
The classic advice says "under 5 minutes," but that's TV-agent-era. For employer/brand roles
in 2026, a **60–90 second** reel that proves range wins — hiring managers skim. Structure:
- **0:00–0:10** — your single best on-camera moment, full energy, talking to camera.
- **0:10–0:60** — 4–6 fast cuts showing RANGE: a direct-to-camera ad/spokesperson read, a
  product demo/explainer, a hosting/interview moment, a short-form (TikTok/Reel) piece.
- Each clip **10–30 seconds**. Cut before it drags. Personality > polish.
- End card: name · "On-camera spokesperson & host" · videorep.co · email.

You already have this at **videorep.co** — the job is to make sure the FIRST clip is your
strongest and it opens with you talking to camera, not a logo or slate.

### 2. Format-specific "proof" clips employers ask for
Keep these as standalone links so you can drop the right one into an application. Map to your
4 buckets:
| Bucket | Clip to have ready |
|---|---|
| **Sales Funnel** | A 30–60s direct-response ad / UGC-style read (problem → product → CTA). |
| **Content Marketing** | A YouTube/educational segment showing you can hold a topic and be watchable. |
| **Product & Training** | A crisp product demo or explainer — you making something complex feel easy. |
| **General Spokesperson** | A polished brand-host/spokesperson read to camera, teleprompter-smooth. |

If any bucket is thin, **shoot one this week** from a public script (see below). One clean
new clip per bucket makes you applicable to 4x more roles.

### 3. A simple portfolio page (you have videorep.co)
Employers want ONE link that shows: the reel up top, then the 4 bucket clips, a one-line bio,
and contact/booking. Keep it skimmable. No login walls.

### 4. Headshot + one-line bio
Some ATS/application forms want a still and a short bio. Have a current, well-lit headshot and
the summary line from your resume ready to paste.

## What makes an on-camera reel "get hired" (the qualities they score)
From the research, in order of what they look for:
1. **Personality / presence** — the #1 thing agents cite. Be conversational, natural, warm.
2. **Range** — anchor/host, report/present, demo, story-tell. Show you're not one-note.
3. **Teleprompter & direct-to-camera ease** — no drift, eyes locked, natural cadence.
4. **Energy & confidence** — start strong, speak with feeling about the content.
5. **Storytelling / writing** — a produced feature or scripted piece shows you can shape a
   message, not just read one.

---

## How to shoot a fresh bucket clip fast (no client needed)
1. Grab a real, public script: a brand's product page, a SaaS feature, a nonprofit's mission.
2. Write a 30–60s version (problem → what it does → why it matters → CTA).
3. Shoot on the A7III, teleprompter, clean light/audio. One strong take beats ten okay ones.
4. Cut to 30–45s, add captions (most are watched muted), end card with your contact.
5. Post unlisted/public and add the link to videorep.co + your application toolkit.

---

## Common mistakes to avoid
- **Opening on a slate/logo/countdown** — you've lost them. Open on YOU.
- **Too long** — 5 min of "everything" reads as "no highlight reel." Cut ruthlessly.
- **All one format** — 6 versions of the same read = looks like limited range.
- **No captions** — muted autoplay is the default; uncaptioned = skipped.
- **Stale links** — check every portfolio link monthly (the same discipline that keeps job
  links from 404ing).
