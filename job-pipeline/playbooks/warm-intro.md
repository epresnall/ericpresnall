# Warm-intro playbook — recently-closed roles

For companies whose on-camera posting already filled. **Why it works:** they've proven demand
*and* budget for video. Even if filled: new hires don't always stick, one hire rarely covers all
a brand's video, and you become first-in-line for the overflow and the next opening. High signal,
almost zero competition.

### Message 1 — soft "raise my hand" (LinkedIn DM or email)
> Hi [Name] — saw [Company] was hiring an on-camera host for [role]. Whether or not that seat's filled, figured I'd raise my hand. I'm a video spokesperson and host — 20 years on camera, 500+ brand videos, Animal Planet and Broadway along the way. If you ever need overflow, a second face, or someone to take video off your plate end to end, I'd love to be on your radar. Reel 👉 https://videorep.co — no pitch, just planting a flag.

### Message 2 — "you clearly invest in video" (→ VideoRep partner)
> Hi [Name] — [Company]'s clearly leaning into video (saw the [role] opening). Most brands that hire one on-camera person quickly find they need more content than one role can cover — that's usually where I come in: one presenter plus a full production team, so ads, social, and product videos all come from one place. If that's ever useful, here's my reel 👉 https://videorep.co — happy to record a free sample from any script.

### Message 3 — light follow-up (~1 week later, then stop)
> Hey [Name] — floating this back up in case it's useful down the line. Filing me under "on-camera, whenever you need it" is all I'm after. Reel's here if it helps 👉 https://videorep.co

### Who to message
- The person who posted the role, or the **Head of Marketing / Content / Brand / Social**.
- Find them on LinkedIn: search "[Company] marketing / content." No reply? Try [firstname]@[company].com.
- One soft follow-up, then move on. Keep a simple list: company · closed role · who · date · reply.
